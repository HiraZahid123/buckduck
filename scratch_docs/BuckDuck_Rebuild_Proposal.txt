1. Executive Summary
The current BuckDuck prototype proves the business model: one cart across many specialty stores, a weekly Saturday delivery window, stores packing in BuckDuck bags, and drivers delivering batched routes. It is a proof of concept, though, and it is not built to take real customers, real payments or Friday-night traffic.
We propose rebuilding the platform from scratch on a production stack: Next.js, NestJS, PostgreSQL and Stripe. The rebuild covers the full marketplace for customers, stores, drivers and operations. It also includes the items a live business cannot launch without: password reset, email/SMS/push notifications, real-time order alerts for stores, real Google Maps navigation for drivers, fast photo uploads, backups and a privacy flow.

2. Why We Are Rebuilding, Not Patching
The existing codebase is a small prototype: Python services on a single-file SQLite database with a vanilla-JavaScript front end. Patching it would cost more than rebuilding it properly, and it would carry these risks into production:
Invisible to Google: the signed-in app renders in the browser, and SEO depends on hand-written templates.
Silent orders: stores get no alerts, chimes or push notifications and must refresh the page.
Friday rush failures: SQLite allows one write at a time, so simultaneous checkouts lock up.
Simulated routing: the driver route line is generated, not real map data.
Blocking uploads: photo uploads freeze the server for other users.
Missing launch basics: no password reset, email verification, notifications, backups or audit log.
We will reuse what the prototype got right: the business rules, pricing formulas, order lifecycle and screen flows.

3.3 Payments & payouts (Stripe)
Card, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Link through Stripe Payment Element; 3-D Secure
Webhook-confirmed payments; no customer is ever charged without an order being created
Full and partial refunds, capped at the amount paid
Stripe Connect Express onboarding for stores; the store share is transferred automatically on delivery; transfers are held until the store is connected

Order page with status, progress timeline, driver name and vehicle, receipt; customer can cancel with a full refund until the store accepts

Store application and approval flow
Dashboard: order counts, bags needed for Saturday, weekly sales, earnings, top products, low-stock alerts
Real-time order board (New → Accepted → Packing → Ready) with an audible chime and browser push for new paid orders

Driver application, online/offline switch, push alerts for new jobs

3.7 Admin console
Overview: GMV, net revenue, orders, average order value, Saturday pipeline, capacity, top stores, payment mix, alerts
Orders: search, filter, assign driver, override status, refunds with reason
Live dispatch board; stores (approve, suspend, commission); users; drivers; delivery windows
Finance settlement by delivery day; all business settings (fees, commission, driver pay, windows, ZIPs) editable without code

Transactional email (receipts, password reset, order updates) and SMS updates at placed, ready, out for delivery and delivered
Real-time updates for the merchant board, dispatch board and customer tracking
Web push notifications for stores and drivers

4.1 Architecture approach
We will build one NestJS application with strictly separated modules: identity, catalog, orders, payments, delivery and notifications. They share one PostgreSQL database. This "modular monolith" keeps clear boundaries between parts of the business with far less hosting, deployment and debugging overhead than separate microservices. That makes it faster to build, cheap to run on a single VPS, and easier to maintain. Background queues keep slow work (emails, payouts, image processing) off the customer's request. Any module can be split into its own service later if scale requires it.

9. Assumptions & Client Responsibilities
Scope is as described in Section 3. Changes go through a simple change-request process.
BuckDuck provides feedback quickly: design approvals and UAT feedback within 1 business day.
BuckDuck owns and provides access to third-party accounts: Hostinger, Stripe (with live approval), Google Cloud, Twilio, Resend or SMTP mailbox, Cloudflare, domain and Apple Pay domain verification.
BuckDuck provides branding assets, legal texts and initial store and product data, or pilot stores enter their own.
The prototype is used as a functional reference only. No code or data is migrated unless agreed.
Launch is one metro (Houston, TX) on a single delivery day, with English-only content and USD.

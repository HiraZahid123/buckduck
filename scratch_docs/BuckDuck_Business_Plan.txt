1. Executive Summary
BuckDuck is a grocery delivery marketplace that works like DoorDash or Uber Eats, with one deliberate difference: delivery happens once a week, on Saturday, inside time windows customers choose in advance — Morning (6 AM–12 PM) or Afternoon (12 PM–4 PM). Independent grocery stores open their own storefronts on the StoreFront USA website and the BuckDuck app, receive orders all week, and pack them in branded BuckDuck bags for driver pickup on delivery day. Customers pay BuckDuck at checkout by card, Apple Pay or Google Pay.
We start where existing apps serve customers worst: African, Caribbean and other specialty grocers. These stores carry products big-box retailers do not stock — gari, palm oil, egusi, stockfish, scotch bonnets, halal goat — but they are rarely on Instacart, and families often drive 30–60 minutes each weekend to reach them. BuckDuck brings those stores to the customer's door in one coordinated weekly run.
Why weekly delivery wins
Concentrating all deliveries into one day lets us batch routes tightly, keep driver pay high per hour, and let stores pack calmly in advance instead of fulfilling one-off orders all week. Customers already shop for staples weekly, so the model fits how they buy. The result is lower cost per delivery than on-demand apps and better margins for everyone.

The opportunity
Online grocery is mainstream. US e-grocery sales reached a record $12.7 billion in December 2025, up 32% year over year, and online now accounts for about 19% of weekly grocery spending (Brick Meets Click).
The community is large and growing. The sub-Saharan African immigrant population in the US tripled since 2000 to about 2.5 million in 2024, led by Nigeria, Ethiopia and Ghana (Migration Policy Institute). Add Caribbean, Latin American and other diaspora shoppers and the addressable audience multiplies.
Specialty grocers are a big, under-digitized channel. US ethnic supermarkets generated an estimated $58.9 billion in 2025 (IBISWorld), yet most independent stores have no e-commerce or delivery.
What is already built
The full platform is built and deployment-ready: a customer website and installable mobile app, a merchant portal, a driver app and an admin console, sharing one backend with Stripe payments (card, Apple Pay, Google Pay), Stripe Connect payouts, delivery-window capacity management and order tracking. Section 9 describes it in detail.

3. Problem & Solution
The problem
For customers
Specialty groceries are scattered across stores that may sit far apart — a Ghanaian market for gari and shito, a halal butcher for goat, a Caribbean shop for ackee.
Mainstream delivery apps rarely list these stores, and big-box substitutes miss the products or the quality.
Weekend shopping trips cost hours of driving, fuel and parking.
For independent grocers
No affordable way to sell online: building a website, payments and delivery is out of reach for most owner-operators.
On-demand apps charge high commissions and push unpredictable one-off orders that disrupt store operations.
Younger, busier customers are drifting to convenience channels the store cannot offer.
For drivers
On-demand gig work means idle time between orders, unpredictable earnings and long waits at restaurants.

Why once a week
Route density. Every order for a neighborhood lands on the same day, so a driver can run 6–8 stops per hour instead of 2–3 on demand.
Calm store operations. An order cutoff 24 hours before each window gives stores a predictable packing schedule and lets them order inventory against known demand.
Habit. Weekly ordering matches how families buy staples, which supports high repeat rates and a natural subscription offer.
Lower prices. Efficiency lets us charge a modest $5.99 delivery fee per store while still paying drivers well.
4. Market Analysis
Industry trends
US online grocery hit a record $12.7 billion in December 2025, up 32% from a year earlier; average order value rose nearly 11% and order frequency increased for the 16th straight month (Brick Meets Click via SDCExec).
Online's share of weekly grocery spend reached about 19%, the highest level since May 2020.
US ethnic supermarkets are an estimated $58.9 billion industry (IBISWorld, 2025) with largely offline operations.
The sub-Saharan African immigrant population reached about 2.5 million in 2024; top origins are Nigeria (533,000), Ethiopia (304,000) and Ghana (223,000). Washington DC, New York, Dallas, Minneapolis and Atlanta hold over a third of this population (Migration Policy Institute).

8. Merchant & Driver Programs
Merchant program
Apply online at StoreFront USA with store details; BuckDuck reviews and approves within 1–2 business days.
Build the storefront: logo, brand color, description, and product listings with photos, prices, units and stock.
Connect payouts through Stripe Connect Express — the store's bank account receives earnings automatically after each delivery.
Receive the packaging kit and a 30-minute training on the order queue and packing standard.
Go live and start receiving orders for the next Saturday.
What stores get
A branded storefront with its own web address
Order queue: accept → pack → ready, with printable pick lists that combine items across orders
Live dashboard: sales by week, top products, low-stock alerts, bags needed for Saturday
Inventory control and product photo uploads
Automatic payouts and a weekly settlement history
Commercial terms
15% commission on merchandise; launch partners receive 10% for their first 90 days
No setup fees and no monthly fees
Stores set their own prices
Driver program
Pay: $5.50 base per delivery plus 100% of tips, paid weekly.
Predictable schedule: Saturdays only, with morning and afternoon shifts.
Batched routes: up to 12 stops per route, with the full route, store pickup and customer drop-off shown in the driver app.
Requirements: valid license, insurance, background check, a vehicle with cargo space, and an insulated bag kit (provided).
Proof of delivery: a drop-off note and optional photo on every order.
Driver classification
Drivers are planned as independent contractors. Contractor rules vary by state (for example California's AB5 and Proposition 22). Have an employment attorney review the driver agreement before launch, and budget for occupational accident coverage.

Architecture
Backend: Node.js 22 and Express, with a shared business-logic core so every rule (pricing, windows, status flow, payouts) lives in one place.
Database: SQLite for launch, with a clear migration path to PostgreSQL as volume grows.
Payments: Stripe PaymentIntents with automatic payment methods, which turns on card, Apple Pay, Google Pay and Link; Stripe Connect Express for store payouts; signed webhooks for payment confirmation.
Mobile: installable progressive web app on iOS and Android today; can be wrapped for the App Store and Google Play with Capacitor.
Security: hashed passwords (bcrypt), signed session tokens, role-based access for all four user types, rate-limited sign-in, strict content security policy; card data never touches BuckDuck servers (PCI scope handled by Stripe).
Deployment: Docker image with a persistent data volume; one-click configuration for Render, Railway or Fly.io.
10. Marketing & Go-to-Market
Phase 1 — Supply first (months 1–3)
Sign 20–35 anchor stores across African, Caribbean, halal meat, produce and bakery categories before customer launch.
Offer launch partners a 10% commission for 90 days, a free packaging kit and in-store signage ("Order us on BuckDuck").
Photograph best-selling products for each store to make storefronts look complete on day one.
Phase 2 — Community launch (months 3–6)
Churches and associations: partner with congregations and national associations (Ghanaian, Nigerian, Ethiopian, Caribbean) for member promotions and Saturday morning group orders.
In-store conversion: every partner store hands out QR cards with a first-order credit.
Referral program: give $10, get $10 for each new customer who completes an order.
Social and creators: short-form video with diaspora food creators showing a full Saturday haul.
Events: booths at cultural festivals, independence-day celebrations and food fairs.
Phase 3 — Habit and expansion (months 6–12)
Launch BuckDuck+ membership and recurring "weekly basket" orders.
Add recipe bundles (Jollof Night, Sunday Stew, Caribbean Brunch) that pull from several stores in one click.
Prepare the second metro using the same playbook.

15. Legal & Compliance
Business formation: Delaware C-Corp with founder vesting, IP assignment and an option pool.
Payments: Stripe as payment facilitator; BuckDuck stores no card data. Stripe Connect handles store identity verification (KYC) and 1099-K reporting.
Sales tax: grocery is exempt or reduced in many states; prepared foods may be taxable. The platform supports a configurable tax rate; confirm marketplace-facilitator obligations per state with a tax advisor.
Food safety: partner stores hold their own retail food permits; BuckDuck publishes and enforces cold-chain handling standards.
Insurance: general liability, commercial auto / hired and non-owned auto, cyber liability and occupational accident coverage for drivers.
Terms and privacy: customer terms, merchant agreement, driver agreement and privacy policy (CCPA-ready).
Trademarks: file for BuckDuck and StoreFront USA marks and secure matching domains and social handles.

1. Introduction
1.1 Purpose
This document defines what the BuckDuck platform must do for the business, for each type of user, and for operations. It is the reference for development, testing, vendor quotes and launch sign-off. Each requirement has an ID so it can be traced to code, tests and acceptance.
1.2 Product summary
BuckDuck is a grocery delivery marketplace. Independent grocery stores, starting with African, Caribbean, halal and specialty grocers, open storefronts on the StoreFront USA website and BuckDuck app. Customers order from one or more stores in a single cart and choose a Saturday delivery window: Morning (6 AM–12 PM) or Afternoon (12 PM–4 PM). Stores pack orders in sealed BuckDuck-branded bags. BuckDuck drivers pick up and deliver. All payments go through BuckDuck (card, Apple Pay, Google Pay), and stores are paid out automatically.

2. Business Context & Objectives
2.1 Problem
Customers must visit several specialty stores, often far apart, to buy the foods they cook with. Mainstream delivery apps rarely list these stores.
Independent grocers have no affordable way to sell online, take card and wallet payments, or offer delivery.
Drivers on on-demand apps lose time between orders and earn unpredictably.
2.2 Solution
One weekly delivery day lets BuckDuck batch routes, give stores a calm packing schedule, and charge customers less per delivery while paying drivers well. A shared platform gives every store a storefront, payments and delivery without building their own.

13. Assumptions, Constraints & Dependencies
Assumptions
Launch is in one metro (demo configured for Houston, TX) with 20–35 stores before public launch.
Partner stores have a smartphone or computer to use the merchant portal and can pack to the BuckDuck standard.
Drivers are independent contractors with their own vehicles; BuckDuck supplies insulated bag kits.
Customers accept a once-a-week delivery in exchange for authentic products and lower fees.
Constraints
Apple Pay works only over HTTPS on a verified domain, in Safari and iOS.
Stripe Connect requires stores to complete identity verification before payouts.
Driver classification and sales-tax rules vary by state and must be reviewed by advisors before each new market.
Seed budget of $450,000 (see Business Plan).
Dependencies
Stripe account approval and live keys.
A domain name and HTTPS hosting.
PostgreSQL hosting with daily backups.
Email and SMS providers for Phase 2 notifications.
